1 — Wave Properties
f — frequency (Hz) | λ — wavelength (m) | T — period (s) | v — wave speed (m/s) | A — amplitude (m)
n — refractive index | I — intensity (W/m²) | P — power (W) | r — distance from source (m)
2 — Wave Equations
Given
Wave equation
v = fλ
Period-frequency relationship
T = 1/f
Speed on a string
v = √(T/μ)
T — tension in string (N) | μ — mass per unit length (kg/m)
Must Memorise
Intensity
I = P / (4πr²)
I — intensity (W/m²) | P — power of source (W) | r — distance from point source (m)
Key Result
Intensity ∝ amplitude² — doubling amplitude quadruples intensity
3 — Refraction and Total Internal Reflection
Given
Refractive index
n = c / v
Snell's Law
n1sinθ1 = n2sinθ2
n — refractive index | c — speed of light in vacuum (3.00 × 10⁸ m/s) | v — speed in medium | θ — angle from normal
Must Memorise
Critical Angle
sin C = 1/n
C — critical angle (°) | n — refractive index (denser medium)
Note: TIR occurs when angle of incidence > critical angle, light travels from denser to less dense medium.
4 — Diffraction and Interference
Given
Double-slit fringe spacing
w = λD / s
w — fringe spacing (m) | λ — wavelength (m) | D — slit-to-screen distance (m) | s — slit separation (m)
Given
Diffraction grating
nλ = d sinθ
n — order number (integer) | d — slit spacing (m) | θ — angle of diffraction (°)
Note: d = 1/N where N = number of lines per metre. Maximum order: n_max = d/λ (truncate to integer).
5 — Stationary Waves
Key Result
Fundamental: f₁ = v/(2L) — string fixed at both ends
Harmonics: fₙ = nf₁ — nth harmonic
Pipe (closed at one end): f₁ = v/(4L), only odd harmonics
6 — Photoelectric Effect
Given
Photon energy
E = hf
Einstein's photoelectric equation
hf = φ + ½mv²_max
h — Planck constant (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s) | f — frequency (Hz) | φ — work function (J) | m — electron mass | v_max — maximum electron speed (m/s)
Must Memorise
Threshold frequency
f₀ = φ / h
Given
de Broglie wavelength
λ = h / p
p — momentum (kg m/s) | h — Planck constant
7 — DC Circuits: Current and Charge
Given
Electric current
I = ΔQ / Δt
Drift velocity
I = nAvq
I — current (A) | Q — charge (C) | n — number density of charge carriers (m⁻³) | A — cross-sectional area (m²) | v — drift velocity (m/s) | q — charge on carrier (C)
8 — DC Circuits: Resistance and Resistivity
Given
Ohm's Law
V = IR
Resistivity
R = ρL / A
V — potential difference (V) | I — current (A) | R — resistance (Ω) | ρ — resistivity (Ω m) | L — length (m) | A — cross-sectional area (m²)
Series: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + ... — same current, V splits
Parallel: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + ... — same V, current splits
9 — DC Circuits: EMF and Internal Resistance
Must Memorise
EMF and terminal voltage
ε = V + Ir
ε — EMF (V) | V — terminal potential difference (V) | I — current (A) | r — internal resistance (Ω)
Key Result
"Lost volts" = Ir — the p.d. lost across internal resistance
V-I graph: gradient = −r, y-intercept = ε
10 — DC Circuits: Energy and Power
Given
Electrical energy
W = VIt
Power (3 forms)
P = VI = I²R = V²/R
W — energy (J) | V — p.d. (V) | I — current (A) | t — time (s) | R — resistance (Ω)
11 — Potential Dividers
Given
Potential divider
V_out = V_in × R₂ / (R₁ + R₂)
V_out — output voltage (V) | V_in — input voltage (V) | R₁, R₂ — resistances (Ω)
Note: LDR: R decreases as light intensity increases. NTC thermistor: R decreases as temperature increases.
