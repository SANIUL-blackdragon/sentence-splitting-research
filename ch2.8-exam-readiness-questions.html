Spec Point 9.1 — Collision Theory and Factors Affecting Rate

Q1 QFR §9.1 2 marks · ~4 min

State the two conditions that must be satisfied for a collision between reactant particles to result in a reaction.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The particles must collide with sufficient energy (equal to or greater than the activation energy) and with the correct orientation.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: sufficient energy (≥ activation energy)
  • 1 mark: correct orientation
Common Error

Students sometimes write "high enough concentration" or "enough particles" — these are not conditions for an individual collision to be successful, they are factors that affect how many collisions occur overall.

Q2 TDC §9.1 2 marks · ~4 min

A student increases the concentration of a reactant in solution. State whether this change affects (i) the frequency of collisions, and (ii) the proportion of collisions that are successful.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(i) The frequency of collisions increases / there are more particles per unit volume leading to more frequent collisions. (ii) The proportion of successful collisions remains the same / does not change.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: frequency of collisions increases
  • 1 mark: proportion of successful collisions stays the same / is unchanged
Common Error

Students often state that "more collisions are successful" when they mean the total number of successful collisions increases. The proportion does not change — only the total frequency does. This distinction is critical in mark schemes.

Q3 STE §9.1 2 marks · ~4 min

State and explain the effect of increasing the surface area of a solid reactant on the rate of reaction.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Increasing the surface area increases the rate of reaction. This is because more particles of the solid are exposed to the other reactant(s), increasing the frequency of collisions between the solid surface and the other reactant particles.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: rate increases
  • 1 mark: explanation in terms of frequency of collisions increasing (must reference more particles exposed / more surface area available) Accept Alternatives: "More particles are available for collisions at any one time"; "greater area for collisions to occur."
Q4 TDC §9.1 3 marks · ~6 min

A student writes: "Increasing the pressure of a gaseous reaction mixture increases the energy of the gas particles, so more collisions are successful and the rate increases." Evaluate this statement by identifying what is correct and what is incorrect.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The statement is partially correct in that increasing pressure does increase the rate of reaction. However, the reasoning is incorrect: increasing pressure does not increase the energy of the particles. Instead, increasing pressure means there are more particles per unit volume, which increases the frequency of collisions. The proportion of successful collisions remains unchanged because the energy distribution of the particles has not changed.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: identifies that the conclusion (rate increases) is correct
  • 1 mark: identifies that the reasoning (energy of particles increases) is incorrect
  • 1 mark: correct explanation — more particles per unit volume → increased frequency of collisions → proportion unchanged
Common Error

This is a high-frequency exam trap. Students confuse the effect of pressure (changes frequency only) with the effect of temperature (changes both frequency and proportion of successful collisions).

Q5 EXP §9.1 3 marks · ~6 min

Explain why increasing the temperature of a reaction mixture generally has a much greater effect on the rate of reaction than increasing the concentration of a reactant by the same proportion.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Increasing temperature increases the frequency of collisions (because particles move faster) and also increases the proportion of collisions that are successful (because a greater proportion of molecules have energy ≥ activation energy). In contrast, increasing concentration only increases the frequency of collisions but has no effect on the proportion of successful collisions. Therefore, temperature affects both factors that determine rate, giving it a greater overall effect.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: temperature increases frequency of collisions (particles move faster)
  • 1 mark: temperature also increases proportion of successful collisions
  • 1 mark: concentration only affects frequency, not proportion Accept Alternatives: "Temperature has an exponential effect on rate (roughly doubles per 10 K) whereas concentration has a linear/proportional effect."
Q6 STE §9.1 2 marks · ~4 min

The concentration of hydrochloric acid in the reaction between calcium carbonate and hydrochloric acid is halved, while all other conditions remain the same. State and explain the effect on: (a) the initial rate of reaction, (b) the final volume of carbon dioxide collected.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(a) The initial rate is halved. Fewer acid particles per unit volume means fewer successful collisions per second. (b) The final volume of gas is halved. There are fewer moles of acid available to react, so fewer moles of CO₂ are produced.

Mark Scheme
  • (a) 1 mark: initial rate is halved + explanation (fewer particles per unit volume / fewer collisions)
  • (b) 1 mark: final volume is halved + explanation (fewer moles of acid / limiting reagent)
Common Error

Students may correctly identify that the rate decreases but state the final volume is "unchanged." Since HCl is the limiting reagent, halving its concentration (and therefore amount if volume is constant) halves the total gas produced.

Q7 SYN §9.1 4 marks · ~8 min

Use collision theory to explain fully how increasing the temperature increases the rate of a chemical reaction. Your answer should distinguish clearly between the effect on collision frequency and the effect on the proportion of successful collisions.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:
  • Increasing temperature causes particles to move faster / have greater average kinetic energy (1 mark).
  • This increases the frequency of collisions between reactant particles, so more collisions occur per unit time (1 mark).
  • In addition, increasing temperature means a greater proportion of molecules have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy (1 mark).
  • Therefore, a greater proportion of collisions are successful, further increasing the rate (1 mark).
  • Since both the frequency of collisions and the proportion of successful collisions increase, the rate of reaction increases significantly.
Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: particles have more energy / move faster / greater average kinetic energy
  • 1 mark: frequency of collisions increases
  • 1 mark: greater proportion of molecules with energy ≥ Eₐ
  • 1 mark: proportion of successful collisions increases Note: A mark for the final "both factors" conclusion is not required separately if all four marking points are made. The "significantly" or "greater effect than concentration alone" is a consequence, not a separate mark.
Q8 SYN §9.1 3 marks · ~6 min

Both increasing pressure (for a gaseous reaction) and increasing temperature increase the rate of reaction. Compare the mechanisms by which each of these changes affects the rate, stating one key similarity and one key difference.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:
  • Similarity: Both changes increase the frequency of collisions (pressure: more particles per unit volume; temperature: particles move faster) (1 mark).
  • Difference: Increasing temperature also increases the proportion of successful collisions (greater proportion of molecules have energy ≥ activation energy), whereas increasing pressure has no effect on the proportion of successful collisions (2 marks).
Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: both increase frequency of collisions (with brief explanation of mechanism for each)
  • 2 marks: difference — temperature changes proportion of successful collisions; pressure does not Accept Alternatives for Similarity: "Both result in a greater number of successful collisions per second."

Spec Point 9.2 — Activation Energy

Q9 QFR §9.2 1 mark · ~2 min

Define the term activation energy.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The minimum energy that particles must have for a collision to be successful (to result in a reaction).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: minimum energy for a successful collision / for a reaction to occur Accept Alternatives: "The minimum energy needed to break bonds and start the reaction"; "the energy barrier that must be overcome for reactants to form products."
Q10 WTE §9.2 2 marks · ~4 min

When asked why increasing temperature increases the rate of a reaction, a common incorrect answer is: "Increasing temperature increases the activation energy." (i) What is wrong with this answer? (ii) What should the correct reasoning be?

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(i) Activation energy is an intrinsic property of a reaction and does not change with temperature. (ii) Increasing temperature means a greater proportion of molecules have energy ≥ the activation energy, so more frequent successful collisions occur per unit time.

Mark Scheme
  • (i) 1 mark: identifies that activation energy does NOT change / is fixed / is intrinsic
  • (ii) 1 mark: correct explanation — greater proportion of molecules have energy ≥ activation energy
Common Error

This is one of the most common student errors in kinetics. Students often conflate the activation energy threshold with the average kinetic energy of the particles. The threshold stays the same; it is the distribution of particle energies that changes.

Q11 STE §9.2 1 mark · ~2 min

State the effect, if any, of increasing the temperature on the activation energy of a reaction.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

No effect / the activation energy is unchanged. (Activation energy is an intrinsic property of the reaction and does not depend on temperature.)

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: no effect / unchanged / does not change Note: If a student writes "increases," this scores zero. Examiners specifically test this distinction.
Q12 TDC §9.2 2 marks · ~4 min

When the temperature of a reaction mixture is increased, state the effect on each of the following: (i) the activation energy, (ii) the most probable energy of the molecules, (iii) the mean energy of the molecules.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(i) No change / remains the same. (ii) Increases / shifts to a higher value. (iii) Increases.

Mark Scheme
  • (i) 1 mark: no change / unchanged
  • (ii) + (iii): 1 mark for both correct (both increase)
Common Error

Students may state that the activation energy decreases at higher temperature, or that it increases. Neither is correct — Eₐ is invariant with temperature for a given reaction.

Q13 SYN §9.2 3 marks · ~6 min

Both increasing temperature and adding a catalyst increase the rate of a chemical reaction. Compare the mechanisms by which each achieves this increase. Your answer must include similarities and differences.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:
  • Similarity: Both result in a greater proportion of molecules having energy ≥ the activation energy, leading to more successful collisions per unit time (1 mark).
  • Difference (temperature): Increasing temperature changes the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution (peak lowers, shifts right) but the activation energy itself remains unchanged; more molecules now exceed the unchanged Eₐ (1 mark).
  • Difference (catalyst): Adding a catalyst provides an alternative pathway with a lower activation energy; the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution remains completely unchanged, but the threshold (Eₐ) is lowered so more molecules exceed it (1 mark).
Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: similarity — both increase proportion of molecules with E ≥ Eₐ
  • 1 mark: difference — temperature changes the distribution; Eₐ unchanged
  • 1 mark: difference — catalyst lowers Eₐ; distribution unchanged
Q14 SYN §9.2 4 marks · ~8 min

Chloromethane (CH₃Cl) hydrolyses more slowly than bromomethane (CH₃Br) under identical conditions. Explain this difference in terms of bond enthalpy and activation energy.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The C–Cl bond has a higher bond enthalpy (is stronger) than the C–Br bond. Therefore, more energy is required to break the C–Cl bond. The activation energy for the hydrolysis of CH₃Cl is higher than for CH₃Br. At any given temperature, a smaller proportion of CH₃Cl molecules have energy ≥ the (higher) activation energy, so fewer successful collisions occur per unit time and the rate of reaction is slower.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: C–Cl bond has a higher bond enthalpy / is stronger
  • 1 mark: more energy needed to break C–Cl / higher activation energy for CH₃Cl
  • 1 mark: smaller proportion of CH₃Cl molecules have energy ≥ activation energy
  • 1 mark: therefore slower rate / fewer successful collisions Accept Alternatives: "C–Cl bond enthalpy is 346 kJ mol⁻¹ and C–Br bond enthalpy is 290 kJ mol⁻¹" (specific values accepted if approximately correct).

Spec Point 9.3 — Measuring Rates of Reaction

Q15 QFR §9.3 1 mark · ~2 min

State how the instantaneous rate of reaction at a particular time is determined from a concentration–time graph.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The instantaneous rate is equal to the gradient of the tangent to the curve at that time.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: gradient of the tangent (at that point / at that time) Accept Alternatives: "Draw a tangent and calculate its gradient"; "the slope of the tangent line."
Q16 WTE §9.3 2 marks · ~4 min

A student is asked to find the rate of reaction at a specific point on a volume–time graph. The student divides the total volume of gas produced by the total time taken. Identify the error in the student's method and state the correct method.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The student has calculated the average rate over the whole time period (1 mark). To find the rate at a specific point, a tangent should be drawn to the curve at that point and the gradient of the tangent calculated (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: identifies the error — student calculated average rate, not instantaneous rate
  • 1 mark: states correct method — draw tangent, calculate its gradient
Common Error

Students frequently confuse average rate with instantaneous rate. Average rate = total change / total time. Instantaneous rate = gradient of tangent at a specific point.

Q17 CAL §9.3 2 marks · ~4 min

In an experiment to compare reaction rates, the time taken for a precipitate to first appear was measured. At 20 °C the time was 120 s and at 40 °C the time was 30 s. Calculate the ratio of the approximate rate at 40 °C to the approximate rate at 20 °C.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Rate at 20 °C ∝ 1/120 = 8.33 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹. Rate at 40 °C ∝ 1/30 = 3.33 × 10⁻² s⁻¹. Ratio = (1/30) / (1/120) = 120/30 = 4.0. The rate at 40 °C is approximately 4 times the rate at 20 °C.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: correct calculation method (rate ∝ 1/time, then ratio = time₁/time₂ = 120/30)
  • 1 mark: correct answer of 4.0 (accept 4 without decimal) Note: The shortcut is simply 120/30 = 4, since rate₁/rate₂ = (1/t₁)/(1/t₂) = t₂/t₁. Students who calculate each rate separately and then divide will also score full marks.
Q18 CAL §9.3 2 marks · ~4 min

In an experiment, the volume of gas produced was measured over time. At time t = 60 s, a tangent was drawn to the curve. The tangent passes through the points (40 s, 12.0 cm³) and (80 s, 28.0 cm³). Calculate the rate of reaction at t = 60 s. Give your answer with appropriate units.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Gradient = (28.0 − 12.0) / (80 − 40) = 16.0 / 40 = 0.40 cm³ s⁻¹ (1 mark) ✓ units: cm³ s⁻¹ (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: correct numerical answer of 0.40 (allow 0.4; must show working for credit if answer is wrong)
  • 1 mark: correct units (cm³ s⁻¹)
Common Error

Students may calculate the gradient upside down as (80 − 40)/(28.0 − 12.0) = 2.5 s cm⁻³, losing the method mark. Always remind students: gradient = Δy/Δx.

Q19 CAL §9.3 3 marks · ~6 min

A student investigates the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide and measures the volume of oxygen gas produced. A tangent is drawn to the volume–time curve at 60 s. The tangent passes through the points (20 s, 12 cm³) and (100 s, 48 cm³). Calculate the rate of reaction at 60 s, giving your answer to two significant figures with units.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Gradient = (48 − 12) / (100 − 20) = 36 / 80 = 0.45 cm³ s⁻¹ (to 2 s.f.).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: correct subtraction of values (Δy = 36, Δx = 80)
  • 1 mark: correct numerical answer (0.45 or 0.45 cm³ s⁻¹)
  • 1 mark: correct units (cm³ s⁻¹) and correct significant figures (2 s.f.)
Common Error

A common error is using the tangent point itself (60 s) in the calculation rather than two points on the tangent line. The tangent line gives the slope at that point.

Q20 CAL §9.3 2 marks · ~4 min

The reaction between aqueous sodium thiosulfate and hydrochloric acid was timed. Without any added substance the reaction took 142 s. With Fe³⁺ ions present the reaction took 58 s. With Zn²⁺ ions present the reaction took 307 s. Use the relationship rate ∝ 1/time to identify which ion acts as a catalyst and justify your answer.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Fe³⁺ acts as a catalyst. The reaction time decreases from 142 s to 58 s, so the rate increases. Zn²⁺ is an inhibitor/poison because the reaction time increases so the rate decreases.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: Fe³⁺ identified as the catalyst (with justification: time decreased / rate increased)
  • 1 mark: Zn²⁺ identified as an inhibitor/poison (with justification: time increased / rate decreased) Accept Alternatives: "Fe³⁺ decreases the time taken, meaning the rate of reaction is faster"; "Zn²⁺ increases the time, meaning it slows the reaction."
Q21 EXP §9.3 3 marks · ~6 min

The initial rate of a reaction can be estimated using the relationship initial rate ∝ 1/time. Explain why this relationship is only an approximation and does not give the true initial rate.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:
  • 1/time assumes the rate is constant throughout the reaction / assumes a straight-line graph
  • In reality the rate decreases over time as reactant concentration decreases
  • The true initial rate is the gradient of a tangent at t = 0, which is steeper than the average rate
Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: 1/time gives an average rate / assumes constant rate
  • 1 mark: rate actually decreases over time (as concentration decreases)
  • 1 mark: true initial rate is gradient of tangent at t = 0 Note: This question tests understanding of the limitations of the 1/time method. It is commonly used in "disappearing cross" experiments where the time for a precipitate to obscure a mark is measured.
Q22 TDC §9.3 2 marks · ~4 min

Compare the two methods used to measure the rate of a reaction: (a) using the relationship rate ∝ 1/time, and (b) calculating the gradient of a tangent to a concentration–time graph.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(a) Rate ∝ 1/time is an approximation that gives an average/relative rate from the start of the reaction to a fixed endpoint (e.g. a colour change). It is used for comparing rates under different conditions. (b) The gradient of a tangent to a concentration–time graph gives the instantaneous rate at a specific point in time. It is more accurate and shows how rate changes as the reaction proceeds.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: describes 1/time — gives average/relative rate; used for comparison
  • 1 mark: describes tangent method — gives instantaneous rate; more accurate Accept Alternatives: "1/time is quicker and simpler but less accurate"; "tangent method requires a graph but gives the exact rate at any moment."
Q23 SYN §9.3 3 marks · ~6 min

The rate of reaction between excess magnesium and dilute hydrochloric acid is monitored by measuring the volume of hydrogen gas produced every 10 seconds. (a) Explain why the rate of reaction decreases as the reaction proceeds. (b) Describe how the rate of reaction at exactly t = 45 s can be determined from the resulting volume–time graph.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(a) The concentration of hydrochloric acid decreases as the reaction proceeds. Fewer acid particles per unit volume leads to fewer collisions per unit time. (b) Draw a tangent to the volume–time curve at t = 45 s and calculate the gradient of this tangent.

Mark Scheme

(a) - 1 mark: concentration of acid decreases (as it is consumed) - 1 mark: fewer particles per unit volume → fewer collisions → lower rate (b) - 1 mark: draw tangent at t = 45 s and calculate gradient

Common Error

In part (a), students may say "magnesium is used up" — since magnesium is in excess, this is not the reason the rate decreases. It is the acid concentration that decreases.

Spec Point 9.4 — Maxwell–Boltzmann Distribution

Q24 QFR §9.4 1 mark · ~2 min

State the point at which a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution curve must begin on a graph of number of molecules against energy.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The origin (0, 0) / zero energy and zero number of molecules.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: the origin / (0, 0)
Common Error

Some students draw the curve starting above the x-axis. The curve must begin at (0, 0) because no molecule has zero kinetic energy, and the probability of having very low energy approaches zero.

Q25 QFR §9.4 1 mark · ~2 min

State what the total area under a single Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution curve represents.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The total number of molecules (in the sample).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: total number of molecules Accept Alternatives: "Total number of particles"; "the total number of molecules in the sample."
Q26 QFR §9.4 1 mark · ~2 min

State the two quantities plotted on the axes of a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution curve.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Energy (or kinetic energy) on the x-axis and number of molecules (or fraction of molecules) on the y-axis.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: both axes correctly identified (energy on x, number/fraction of molecules on y) Note: Both axes must be correct for the mark. "Molecular energy" or "kinetic energy" are both acceptable for the x-axis.
Q27 TDC §9.4 2 marks · ~4 min

State whether each of the following statements about a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution is true or false. Justify each answer. (a) The curve starts at the origin because a small number of molecules have zero kinetic energy. (b) The area under the curve represents the total number of molecules in the sample.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(a) False. The curve starts at the origin but no molecule has zero energy (1 mark). (b) True. The area under the curve represents the total number of molecules (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: (a) false + justification that no molecule has zero energy
  • 1 mark: (b) true
Common Error

For (a), students incorrectly accept the statement because they confuse "the curve approaches the origin" with "molecules have zero energy." The curve approaches but never actually reaches zero on the y-axis at zero energy.

Q28 WTE §9.4 2 marks · ~4 min

A student draws a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution curve that curves downwards and crosses the x-axis, returning to zero at high energies. State the error in the student's diagram and explain why it is incorrect.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The curve should never cross or reach the x-axis / should be asymptotic (1 mark) because there is no maximum energy — theoretically a very small number of molecules can have very high energies (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: identifies the error — curve must be asymptotic / must not cross the x-axis
  • 1 mark: explanation — no maximum energy / small probability of very high energies
Common Error

This is a frequent drawing error. Students sketch the curve returning to zero on the right side. The correct curve approaches zero but never reaches it, reflecting the fact that there is always a non-zero (though tiny) probability of any energy value.

Q29 WTE §9.4 2 marks · ~4 min

When asked to draw a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution at a higher temperature, a student draws a curve that has a higher peak than the original and a larger total area beneath it. Identify two errors in this diagram.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:
  1. The peak at higher temperature should be lower (not higher) and positioned to the right of the original peak (1 mark). 2. The total area under both curves should be the same, representing the same total number of molecules (1 mark).
Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: peak should be lower and shifted right (either feature scores)
  • 1 mark: total area should be the same (same number of molecules)
Common Error

Students instinctively associate "higher temperature" with "bigger" and draw a taller, wider curve. The peak must be LOWER because the same number of molecules is spread over a wider range of energies.

Q30 EXP §9.4 3 marks · ~6 min

Answer:

Dynamic equilibrium is the state in a closed system where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction, and the concentrations of all reactants and products remain constant.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: reference to closed system, equal forward/backward rates, and constant concentrations (all three elements ideally, but "equal rates and constant concentrations" is sufficient for the mark)
Q63 TDC §9.9 2 marks · ~4 min

A student writes: "At dynamic equilibrium, the concentrations of all reactants and products are equal, which is why the macroscopic properties are constant." State whether the first part of this statement is correct and explain your reasoning.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The statement is incorrect. At dynamic equilibrium, the concentrations of reactants and products are constant, not necessarily equal. The forward rate equals the backward rate, so there is no net change in concentration, but the actual concentration values depend on the equilibrium position.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: identifies the statement as incorrect
  • 1 mark: explains that concentrations are constant, not equal
Common Error

This is one of the most frequently tested misconceptions. "Constant" means "not changing" — it does NOT mean "the same as each other."

Q64 TDC §9.9 3 marks · ~6 min

Consider the following three statements about dynamic equilibrium. For each statement, state whether it is true or false, and give a brief explanation. - Statement 1: "At equilibrium, the concentrations of reactants and products are equal." - Statement 2: "At equilibrium, the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the backward reaction." - Statement 3: "Dynamic equilibrium can be established in an open beaker."

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:
  • Statement 1: False. At equilibrium the concentrations of reactants and products are constant, but they are not necessarily equal.
  • Statement 2: True. This is the defining condition of dynamic equilibrium — the forward and backward rates are equal.
  • Statement 3: False. Dynamic equilibrium can only be established in a closed system where no reactants or products can escape.
Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark per correct true/false + explanation (max 3 marks)
Q65 TDC §9.9 2 marks · ~4 min

Compare the rate of the forward reaction and the rate of the backward reaction: (i) At dynamic equilibrium. (ii) Immediately after the concentration of a reactant is increased in a system at equilibrium.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(i) At dynamic equilibrium: rate of forward reaction equals rate of backward reaction. (ii) Immediately after increasing reactant concentration: rate of forward reaction is greater than rate of backward reaction (the system is no longer at equilibrium and will shift to the right to oppose the change).

Mark Scheme
  • (i) 1 mark: forward rate equals backward rate
  • (ii) 1 mark: forward rate greater than backward rate (system no longer at equilibrium) Accept for (ii): "Forward rate increases but backward rate stays the same initially, so forward > backward."
Q66 EXP §9.9 2 marks · ~4 min

Explain why dynamic equilibrium can only be achieved in a closed system.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

In a closed system, no reactants or products can enter or leave. If the system were open, products could escape or reactants could be added, meaning the forward and backward rates would never become equal and concentrations would not remain constant.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: closed system prevents matter entering or leaving
  • 1 mark: in an open system, rates would not become equal / concentrations would not be constant
Q67 EXP §9.9 3 marks · ~6 min

Explain what is meant by a dynamic equilibrium in a closed system. Why does a reversible reaction at dynamic equilibrium never go to completion?

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

In a dynamic equilibrium, the forward and backward reactions occur at equal rates in a closed system, so the concentrations of all species remain constant (1 mark). The reaction never goes to completion because the products can react in the reverse direction (1 mark); as product concentration increases, the backward rate increases until it equals the forward rate, at which point equilibrium is established and no further net change occurs (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: forward and backward reactions occur at equal rates in a closed system
  • 1 mark: products can react in reverse direction
  • 1 mark: backward rate increases as product concentration increases until rates are equal
Q68 STE §9.9 2 marks · ~4 min

At dynamic equilibrium, the concentrations of all reactants and products remain constant. Does this mean the reaction has stopped? Explain your reasoning.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

No, the reaction has not stopped. At dynamic equilibrium, the forward and backward reactions are both still occurring but at the same rate. The concentrations are constant because the rate of formation of each substance equals its rate of consumption — this is why the equilibrium is described as dynamic.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: No / reaction has not stopped
  • 1 mark: both forward and backward reactions still occurring at the same rate Accept Alternatives: "The word 'dynamic' tells us the reactions are still happening"; "individual molecules are still reacting but there is no overall change."

Spec Point 9.10 — Le Chatelier's Principle

Q69 QFR §9.10 2 marks · ~4 min

State Le Chatelier's principle.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

If a system at dynamic equilibrium is subjected to a change, the equilibrium position shifts to oppose (counteract) the imposed change.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: system at equilibrium is subjected to a change
  • 1 mark: equilibrium shifts to oppose / counteract the change Accept Alternatives: "The equilibrium position moves in the direction that tends to reduce the effect of the change."
Q70 STE §9.10 2 marks · ~4 min

State the effect of increasing the total pressure on the equilibrium position for each of the following reactions: (i) N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) (ii) H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(i) Equilibrium shifts to the right / moves towards the product side (fewer gas moles) (1 mark). (ii) No effect / equilibrium position unchanged (equal gas moles on both sides) (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • (i) 1 mark: shifts right / towards products (with reference to fewer gas moles)
  • (ii) 1 mark: no change / no effect (with reference to equal gas moles)
Common Error

Students sometimes say (ii) shifts left or right. With equal moles (2 mol → 2 mol), pressure has NO effect on the equilibrium position.

Q71 TDC §9.10 2 marks · ~4 min

A student states: "Increasing the pressure of any gaseous equilibrium mixture will always increase the yield of products." State whether this is true or false and justify your answer with reference to the number of gas moles on each side of the equilibrium.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

False. Increasing the pressure causes the equilibrium to shift towards the side with fewer gas moles. The yield of products only increases if the product side has fewer gas moles than the reactant side. If the product side has more gas moles, increasing pressure decreases the yield of products.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: false
  • 1 mark: justification — shift is to side with fewer gas moles, not always to products
Q72 TDC §9.10 2 marks · ~4 min

A student states: "Decreasing the temperature of any equilibrium reaction will always increase the yield of product." Evaluate this statement.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The statement is incorrect. Decreasing the temperature only increases the yield of product when the forward reaction is exothermic. If the forward reaction is endothermic, decreasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium to the left (towards reactants), which decreases the yield of product.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: identifies statement as incorrect
  • 1 mark: explains with reference to exothermic vs endothermic direction
Q73 TDC §9.10 2 marks · ~4 min

Compare the effect of decreasing the temperature on the equilibrium yield for the following two reactions. Reaction 1: SO₂(g) + ½O₂(g) ⇌ SO₃(g) ΔH = −192 kJ mol⁻¹ Reaction 2: CH₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H₂(g) ΔH = +206 kJ mol⁻¹

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

For Reaction 1: yield of SO₃ increases / equilibrium shifts to the right (exothermic direction) (1 mark). For Reaction 2: yield of H₂ decreases / equilibrium shifts to the left (exothermic direction) (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: Reaction 1 — yield increases (shifts right / exothermic direction)
  • 1 mark: Reaction 2 — yield decreases (shifts left / exothermic direction)
Common Error

Students may say "decreasing temperature decreases yield for both reactions." This shows they have not applied Le Chatelier's principle correctly — the direction of the temperature shift depends on the enthalpy change of the reaction.

Q74 STE §9.10 2 marks · ~4 min

For the equilibrium CH₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H₂(g), state the effect of increasing the total pressure on (i) the yield of hydrogen, (ii) the rate of reaction.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(i) The yield of hydrogen decreases / equilibrium shifts to the left (side with fewer gas moles) (1 mark). (ii) The rate of reaction increases (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • (i) 1 mark: yield decreases (shifts left / 2 mol gas → 4 mol gas on right)
  • (ii) 1 mark: rate increases Note: Part (ii) tests the distinction between equilibrium position and rate. Increasing pressure ALWAYS increases rate (more collisions) but may increase OR decrease yield depending on gas moles.
Q75 EXP §9.10 2 marks · ~4 min

Sodium hydroxide is added to the equilibrium mixture: HCrO₄⁻(aq) ⇌ CrO₄²⁻(aq) + H⁺(aq). Explain the effect on the equilibrium position.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

NaOH reacts with (removes) H⁺ ions (1 mark). The equilibrium shifts to the right to replace (oppose the removal of) H⁺ / to counteract the change (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: H⁺ is removed (by reaction with NaOH / neutralisation)
  • 1 mark: equilibrium shifts right to oppose / to replace H⁺
Common Error

Students may say "NaOH is added so the equilibrium shifts left" — they incorrectly treat NaOH as if it were increasing the concentration of a product. The key is to identify what NaOH reacts WITH (H⁺) and then apply Le Chatelier's principle to the removal of that species.

Q76 EXP §9.10 3 marks · ~6 min

Bleach solution contains two linked equilibria: - Eq.1: ClO⁻(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ HClO(aq) + OH⁻(aq) - Eq.2: HClO(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) + H⁺(aq) ⇌ Cl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) Explain step-by-step why adding an acid such as HCl to bleach can release toxic chlorine gas. In your answer, use Le Chatelier's principle and state which equilibrium shifts and in which direction at each stage.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Adding HCl provides H⁺ ions. For Eq.1, the H⁺ ions react with OH⁻, reducing [OH⁻], so the equilibrium shifts to the right to oppose the change, producing more HClO (1 mark). The increased [HClO] and the added H⁺ and Cl⁻ (from HCl) mean that for Eq.2, the equilibrium shifts to the right (to oppose the increase in [HClO], [Cl⁻], and [H⁺]), producing Cl₂(aq) which escapes as toxic Cl₂(g) (1 mark). The system responds through two linked equilibrium shifts driven by the initial addition of acid (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: Eq.1 — H⁺ reacts with OH⁻; equilibrium shifts right; more HClO produced
  • 1 mark: Eq.2 — increased [HClO], [H⁺], [Cl⁻]; equilibrium shifts right; Cl₂ produced
  • 1 mark: linked explanation — initial acid addition drives both shifts
Q77 EXP §9.10 2 marks · ~4 min

The steam-reforming process for hydrogen production involves the equilibrium: CH₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H₂(g). Explain why passing excess steam through the reaction mixture increases the yield of hydrogen.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Adding excess steam increases the concentration of H₂O (a reactant) (1 mark). The equilibrium shifts to the right to oppose the change, increasing the yield of hydrogen (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: concentration of H₂O increases (excess steam)
  • 1 mark: equilibrium shifts right to oppose → increased H₂ yield
Q78 SYN §9.10 3 marks · ~6 min

Explain why increasing the total pressure on a gaseous equilibrium mixture always increases the rate of reaction, but may either increase or decrease the equilibrium yield. Use an example to support your answer.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Increasing pressure increases the concentration (of gas molecules) (1 mark), so there are more frequent (successful) collisions per unit time, increasing the rate (1 mark). The effect on yield depends on the number of gas moles on each side: e.g. for N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) increasing pressure increases yield (shifts to side with fewer gas moles), but for CH₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 3H₂(g) increasing pressure decreases yield (shifts to side with fewer gas moles / reactant side) (1 mark).

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: increased pressure → increased concentration → increased rate (always)
  • 1 mark: yield depends on gas moles on each side
  • 1 mark: specific example showing both possibilities
Q79 SYN §9.10 4 marks · ~8 min

For the equilibrium PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g), where the forward reaction is endothermic: (a) State and explain the effect of increasing the temperature on the equilibrium yield of PCl₃. (b) State and explain the effect of increasing the total pressure on the equilibrium yield of PCl₃. (c) State the effect of increasing the temperature on the rate of this reaction.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(a) The equilibrium yield of PCl₃ increases. The forward reaction is endothermic, so increasing the temperature shifts the equilibrium to the right (in the endothermic direction) to oppose the change, by Le Chatelier's principle. (b) The equilibrium yield of PCl₃ decreases. There is 1 mol of gas on the left (PCl₅) and 2 mol of gas on the right (PCl₃ + Cl₂). (c) The rate of reaction increases because molecules have more kinetic energy and a greater proportion of molecules have energy ≥ the activation energy.

  • (a) 1 mark: yield increases + explanation (forward endothermic, shift right)
  • (b) 1 mark: yield decreases + explanation (1 mol → 2 mol gas, shift left)
  • (c) 1 mark: rate increases + explanation (greater proportion with E ≥ Eₐ)
  • 1 mark: overall quality of explanation and use of Le Chatelier's principle

Spec Point 9.11 — Industrial Conditions and Compromise

State what is meant by the term compromise conditions in the context of an industrial chemical process.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Conditions that are a compromise between the conditions that give the maximum yield (from Le Chatelier's principle) and the conditions that give an acceptable rate (from kinetics) and are economically viable.

Mark Scheme
  • 1 mark: reference to balancing yield and rate / economic viability Accept Alternatives: "Conditions that give an acceptable yield at an economically viable rate."

For the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) (ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹), state the effect of increasing the temperature on (i) the yield of ammonia and (ii) the rate of reaction.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(i) The yield of ammonia decreases because increasing temperature favours the endothermic (backward) reaction (shifts equilibrium to the left) (1 mark). (ii) The rate of reaction increases because a greater proportion of molecules have energy ≥ the activation energy (1 mark).
