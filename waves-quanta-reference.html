§ 02

Core wave properties

Frequency & period inversely proportional

f = 1T   ·   T = 1f

  • ffrequency, Hz
  • Tperiod, s

Universal wave equation

v = f λ

  • vwave speed, m s⁻¹
  • ffrequency, Hz
  • λwavelength, m

For a given medium, v is constant. If f is fixed, then v ∝ λ. The bench above runs this equation with v pinned — drag the frequency and λ answers immediately.

§ 03

Intensity, amplitude & the inverse-square law

Intensity definition

I = PA

  • Iintensity, W m⁻²
  • Ppower, W
  • Aarea, m²

Intensity & amplitude ∝ A²

I ∝ A2

  • Aamplitude, m

Doubling amplitude increases intensity by a factor of 4 — try it on the bench: the intensity readout squares whatever you do to A.

Inverse-square law for a point source ∝ 1/r²

I ∝ 1r2   ·   I = P4πr2

  • rdistance from source, m
  • Ppower, W

The 4πr² is the area of a sphere — radiation from a point source spreads over it, so intensity falls with the square of distance.

Effective intensity on an angled surface

Ieff = I cos θ

  • θangle between radiation and the normal to the surface

Efficiency

Efficiency = Useful Power OutputTotal Power Input × 100%

§ 04

Refraction, Snell’s law & total internal reflection

Refractive index n ∝ 1/v

n = cv

  • nrefractive index, dimensionless
  • cspeed of light in vacuum, m s⁻¹
  • vspeed of light in the material, m s⁻¹

Higher n means slower light speed in the material.

Snell’s law

n1 sin θ1 = n2 sin θ2

  • n₁refractive index of incident medium
  • θ₁angle of incidence from the normal
  • n₂refractive index of refracted medium
  • θ₂angle of refraction from the normal

If light enters a denser medium, n2 > n1, so it bends towards the normal.

Critical angle

sin C = n2n1   ·   into air/vacuum (n2 = 1):   sin C = 1n

  • Ccritical angle

Conditions for total internal reflection

  • n1 > n2  — light travels in the denser medium
  • Angle of incidence > C

§ 05

Stationary waves & strings

Mass per unit length

μ = masslength

  • μmass per unit length, kg m⁻¹

Wave speed on a string

v = Tμ

  • Ttension, N
  • μmass per unit length, kg m⁻¹

v ∝ √T  ·  v ∝ 1/√μ — a heavier string plays slower.

Fundamental frequency

f1 = v2L   ·   f1 = 12L Tμ

  • Llength of string, m
  • vwave speed on the string

Harmonics

fn = n f1   ·   λn = 2Ln

  • nharmonic number, 1, 2, 3, …

Each harmonic packs one more half-wavelength into the same string.

Derived chain — fundamental frequency squared chain · §13

f2 = T4L2 μ

  • ffundamental frequency, Hz

Combining v = fλ, λ = 2L and v = √(T/μ). This shows f ∝ 1/L, f ∝ 1/√μ and f ∝ √T.

§ 06

Interference, diffraction & beats

Path difference & phase difference Δφ ∝ Δx

Δφ = 2πλ × Δx

  • Δφphase difference, rad
  • Δxpath difference, m
  • λwavelength, m

Constructive: Δx = nλ  ·  Destructive: Δx = (n + ½)λ, with n = 0, 1, 2, …

Young’s double-slit fringe spacing

w = λ Ds

  • wfringe spacing, m
  • Ddistance from slits to screen, m
  • sslit separation, m

Valid when D ≫ s.

Beat frequency

fbeat = | f1 − f2 |

  • f_beatnumber of loudness variations per second, Hz

Diffraction grating equation

nλ = d sin θ

  • norder number, 0, 1, 2, …
  • λwavelength, m
  • dslit spacing, m
  • θangle from normal

d = 1N   ·   lines per mm:   d = 1N × 103   ·   nmax = dλ

  • Nlines per metre (or per mm)

Maximum order: set θ = 90°, then round down to the nearest integer.

Single-slit diffraction — first minimum

sin θ = λa   ·   θ ≈ 2λa

  • aslit width, m
  • θtotal angular width of central bright fringe, rad

Narrower slit gives a wider central maximum. The ≈ form is valid for small angles.

§ 07

Polarisation

Single filter on unpolarised light

I = I02

  • I₀incident intensity
  • Itransmitted intensity after one filter

This is independent of filter orientation.

Malus’s law — between two filters

I = I0 cos2 θ

  • I₀intensity after the first filter
  • Iintensity after the second filter
  • θangle between the transmission axes of the two filters

§ 08

Pulse-echo & ultrasound

The factor-of-2 trap The pulse travels to the surface and back — the measured time is round-trip, so the distance divides by 2.

d = v t2

  • ddistance to reflecting surface, m
  • vspeed of pulse, m s⁻¹
  • tround-trip time, s

Acoustic impedance

Z = ρ c

  • Zacoustic impedance, kg m⁻² s⁻¹
  • ρdensity, kg m⁻³
  • cspeed of sound in the medium, m s⁻¹

Intensity reflection coefficient

IrIi = (Z2 − Z1Z2 + Z1)2

  • I_rreflected intensity
  • I_iincident intensity
  • Z₁impedance of first medium
  • Z₂impedance of second medium

Larger impedance mismatch gives greater reflection.

§ 09

Photons & the photoelectric effect

Photon energy ∝ f ∝ 1/λ

E = hf = hcλ

  • Ephoton energy, J
  • hPlanck constant
  • ffrequency, Hz
  • λwavelength, m
  • cspeed of light

E ∝ f  ·  E ∝ 1/λ

Work function & threshold frequency

φ = h f0

  • φwork function, J or eV
  • f₀threshold frequency, Hz

Einstein’s photoelectric equation

hf = φ + 12 m vmax2   ·   12 m vmax2 = hf − φ

  • v_maxmaximum photoelectron speed, m s⁻¹

Stopping potential

e Vs = 12 m vmax2 = hf − φ

  • V_sstopping potential, V
  • eelementary charge

Graph of KEmax against frequency has gradient h.

Intensity & photoelectrons

  • Intensity ∝ number of photons per second
  • Intensity ∝ photocurrent
  • Intensity does not affect KEmax

Number of photons chain · §13

N = Total EnergyEphoton = P thf

  • Ppower, W
  • ttime, s

§ 10

Wave-particle duality & the de Broglie wavelength

de Broglie wavelength ∝ 1/p

λ = hp = hm v

  • λde Broglie wavelength, m
  • pmomentum, kg m s⁻¹
  • mparticle mass, kg
  • vparticle velocity, m s⁻¹

Derived chain for accelerated electrons chain · §13

λ = h2meV

  • Vaccelerating voltage, V

Using eV = ½mv², p = mv and λ = h/p.

§ 11

Atomic line spectra

Photon emission / absorption energy

ΔE = Eupper − Elower = hf = hcλ

  • ΔEenergy gap between levels, J

Longest wavelength = smallest ΔE  ·  shortest wavelength = largest ΔE.

Number of possible spectral lines from an excited state

N = n(n − 1)2

  • nprincipal quantum number of highest excited level

Example: n = 4 gives N = 6 possible emitted frequencies.
