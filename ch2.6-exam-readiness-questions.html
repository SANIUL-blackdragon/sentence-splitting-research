§6.1 — Enthalpy Change and Standard Conditions

Q1 QFR §6.1 1 mark · ~2 min

State the standard conditions used for enthalpy changes.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

298 K (or 25 °C) and 100 kPa.

Why this scores full marks

The mark scheme accepts "298 K and 100 kPa" in either order. Writing "25 °C" instead of "298 K" is also accepted. However, "room temperature" alone, "298°K" (incorrect unit notation), "1 atm", "normal conditions", or "STP" would all be rejected — the exact values 298 K and 100 kPa are required.

Q2 QFR §6.1 1 mark · ~2 min

Define the term enthalpy change (ΔH).

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The heat energy change at constant pressure. It is the difference between the enthalpy of the products and the enthalpy of the reactants.

Why this scores full marks

Both parts are required for full marks: (1) "heat energy change at constant pressure" and (2) the description as "the difference between the enthalpy of the products and the enthalpy of the reactants." A student who only writes "energy change" without specifying "at constant pressure" would not score.

Q3 TDC §6.1 1 mark · ~2 min

A student is asked to identify the standard state of bromine at 298 K and 100 kPa. They write Br₂(g). Explain why this is incorrect and state the correct standard state.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Bromine is a liquid at 298 K and 100 kPa, so its correct standard state is Br₂(l), not Br₂(g).

Why this scores full marks

The mark scheme explicitly lists Br₂(l) as an example of a standard state. A common error is assuming all diatomic elements are gases — bromine is a liquid under standard conditions. The student must give the correct physical state with the correct state symbol.

Q4 TDC §6.1 1 mark · ~2 min

Which equation correctly represents the standard enthalpy change of formation of Al₂O₃? A. 4Al(s) + 3O₂(g) → 2Al₂O₃(s) B. 2Al(g) + 1½O(g) → Al₂O₃(s) C. 2Al(s) + 1½O₂(g) → Al₂O₃(s) D. 2Al(s) + 1½O₂(g) → Al₂O₃(l)

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

C. 2Al(s) + 1½O₂(g) → Al₂O₃(s)

Why this scores full marks

The correct answer must satisfy three requirements simultaneously: (1) exactly 1 mol of product formed — option A forms 2 mol Al₂O₃ so is wrong; (2) elements in their standard states — option B uses Al(g) and O(g) instead of Al(s) and O₂(g); (3) correct state symbols for the product — option D has Al₂O₃(l) instead of Al₂O₃(s). Fractional coefficients (1½) are accepted in formation equations.

Q5 TDC §6.1 2 marks · ~4 min

Which one of the following equations correctly represents the standard enthalpy of formation of copper(II) carbonate, CuCO₃? A. 2Cu(s) + 2C(s) + 3O₂(g) → 2CuCO₃(s) B. Cu(s) + C(s) + ³⁄₂O₂(g) → CuCO₃(s) C. Cu(s) + C(s) + ³⁄₂O(g) → CuCO₃(s) D. Cu(s) + C(s) + O₃(g) → CuCO₃(s)

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

B. Cu(s) + C(s) + ³⁄₂O₂(g) → CuCO₃(s)

Why this scores full marks

This is worth 2 marks: (1 mark) for identifying option B; (1 mark) for the justification. Option B is the only equation where all reactants are elements in their standard states (Cu, C, and O₂), exactly 1 mol of compound product is formed, and the equation is balanced. Option A forms 2 mol of product — standard enthalpy of formation is always per mole of product. Option C uses O(g) instead of O₂(g) — oxygen's standard state is O₂(g), not O(g). Option D uses O₃(g) which is not the standard state of oxygen.

Q6 STE §6.1 2 marks · ~4 min

A student writes the following equation for the standard enthalpy change of formation of ethanol (C₂H₅OH): 2C(g) + 3H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → C₂H₅OH(l) Identify the error and write the corrected equation. (2 marks)

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The student has written C(g) instead of the correct standard state of carbon. The standard state of carbon is C(s) (solid graphite), not C(g). The corrected equation is: 2C(s) + 3H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → C₂H₅OH(l)

Why this scores full marks

Mark 1 is for identifying the error: carbon must be in its standard state C(s), not C(g). This mirrors the trap highlighted where O₂(g) not O(g) is required — the same principle applies to all elements. Mark 2 is for writing the corrected equation with the proper standard state. Using atoms instead of elements in their standard states scores zero.

Q7 SYN §6.1 5 marks · ~10 min

An exam question asks a student to write the equation for the standard enthalpy change of formation of nitrogen monoxide, NO(g). The student's working is shown below: Step 1: "Standard conditions are room temperature and 1 atm." Step 2: "The equation is: N(g) + O(g) → NO(g)" The examiner awards 0 marks. (a) State the correct standard conditions. (1 mark) (b) Identify two errors in the student's equation and explain why each loses a mark. (2 marks) (c) Write the correct equation for the standard enthalpy change of formation of NO(g). (2 marks)

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

(a) 298 K (or 25 °C) and 100 kPa. (b) Error 1: N(g) is incorrect — nitrogen's standard state is N₂(g), not N(g). Elements must be in their standard states for a formation equation. Error 2: O(g) is incorrect — oxygen's standard state is O₂(g), not O(g). Additionally, the equation is unbalanced as written. (c) ½N₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → NO(g)

Why this scores full marks

Part (a): 1 mark for stating 298 K and 100 kPa — "room temperature" and "1 atm" are both rejected. Part (b): 2 marks — 1 mark for identifying N(g) should be N₂(g) (elemental nitrogen is diatomic) and 1 mark for identifying O(g) should be O₂(g) (elemental oxygen is diatomic). Part (c): 2 marks — 1 mark for correct reactants as elements in standard states with state symbols, 1 mark for correctly balanced equation forming exactly 1 mol of NO(g). Fractional coefficients are accepted.

§6.2 — Exothermic and Endothermic Reactions

Q8 QFR §6.2 1 mark · ~2 min

Define an exothermic reaction.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

A reaction in which heat energy is transferred to the surroundings (ΔH is negative).

Why this scores full marks

The key phrase required by the mark scheme is "heat energy is transferred to the surroundings" (or "heat energy is given out to the surroundings" / "heat is released to the surroundings"). Mentioning that ΔH is negative earns credit if the definition is incomplete, but is not a substitute for the core definition. Simply stating "ΔH is negative" without the heat transfer description is unlikely to score full marks.

Q9 TDC §6.2 1 mark · ~2 min

Which of the following processes is exothermic? A. Cl₂(g) → 2Cl(g) B. Cl(g) → Cl⁺(g) + e⁻ C. 2Cl(g) → Cl₂(g) D. Cl₂(g) → 2Cl⁺(g) + 2e⁻

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

C. 2Cl(g) → Cl₂(g)

Why this scores full marks

Option C is the only exothermic process because it involves bond formation. When a Cl–Cl bond forms, energy is released to the surroundings. Options A, B, and D are all endothermic: A involves bond breaking, B involves ionisation energy (removing an electron), and D involves both bond breaking and ionisation — all of which require energy input.

Q10 EXP §6.2 2 marks · ~4 min

Explain, in terms of bonds, why bond formation is always exothermic.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

During bond formation, attractive electrostatic forces are established between atoms, and energy is released as the new bond is formed. The bonded state has lower potential energy than the separated atoms, so the excess energy is transferred to the surroundings as heat.

Why this scores full marks

Mark 1 is awarded for the idea that new bonds/forming bonds release energy (or that the bonded state is more stable/lower in energy). Mark 2 is awarded for the explanation in terms of electrostatic attraction being established (or that energy is transferred to the surroundings). Simply stating "bond forming is exothermic" without explanation would score zero. The key is explaining WHY — the electrostatic attraction between nuclei and shared electrons lowers the energy of the system.

§6.3 — Enthalpy Level Diagrams

Q11 QFR §6.3 1 mark · ~2 min

State the label that must be used for the vertical axis of an enthalpy level diagram in an Edexcel IAL Chemistry exam.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

enthalpy

Why this scores full marks

The mark scheme for Jun 2022 Q18(a)(iii) explicitly penalised any label other than "enthalpy". Answers of "energy", "energy change", "ΔH", or "heat energy" all score zero. The single word "enthalpy" (case-insensitive) is the only acceptable label.

Q12 TDC §6.3 2 marks · ~4 min

Distinguish between an enthalpy level diagram and a reaction profile. State one key feature that is present on a reaction profile but must NOT be drawn on an enthalpy level diagram.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

An enthalpy level diagram shows only the start and end enthalpy as two horizontal lines with a single arrow between them. A reaction profile shows the full energy pathway from reactants to products, including the activation energy "hump" and possibly an intermediate. The activation energy hump must NOT be drawn on an enthalpy level diagram unless the question specifically asks for a reaction profile or activation energy diagram.

Why this scores full marks

Mark 1 is awarded for correctly describing the difference: enthalpy level diagrams show only start and end points (two lines, one arrow) whereas reaction profiles show the full pathway with energy barriers. Mark 2 is awarded for identifying activation energy as the feature that must not appear on an enthalpy level diagram.

Q13 WTE §6.3 3 marks · ~6 min

Describe, in detail, the enthalpy level diagram you would draw for the complete combustion of octane, C₈H₁₈(l), given that ΔH꜀ = −5461 kJ mol⁻¹.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

A vertical y-axis is drawn and labelled "enthalpy". A horizontal line is drawn near the top of the diagram, labelled "Reactants: C₈H₁₈(l) + 12½O₂(g)". A second horizontal line is drawn significantly lower than the reactants line (because the reaction is highly exothermic), labelled "Products: 8CO₂(g) + 9H₂O(l)". A single-headed vertical arrow is drawn from the reactants line down to the products line, and this arrow is labelled "ΔH꜀ = −5461 kJ mol⁻¹". No activation energy hump is drawn.

Why this scores full marks

Mark 1: Y-axis correctly labelled "enthalpy" (not "energy" or "ΔH"). Mark 2: Reactants line above products line (exothermic reaction), both correctly labelled with species and state symbols. Mark 3: Single-headed arrow from reactants to products correctly labelled with the appropriate subscript ΔH꜀ and the negative value.

Q14 TDC §6.3 2 marks · ~4 min

A student draws an enthalpy level diagram for the complete combustion of methane, ΔH꜀ = −890 kJ mol⁻¹. Which of the following descriptions of the arrow on the diagram is correct? A. A double-headed vertical arrow pointing from products to reactants, labelled −ΔH B. A single-headed vertical arrow pointing from reactants down to products, labelled ΔH꜀ = −890 kJ mol⁻¹ C. A single-headed vertical arrow pointing from products up to reactants, labelled ΔH꜀ = +890 kJ mol⁻¹ D. A double-headed vertical arrow between reactants and products, labelled −ΔH꜀ = −890 kJ mol⁻¹

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

B. A single-headed vertical arrow pointing from reactants down to products, labelled ΔH꜀ = −890 kJ mol⁻¹

Why this scores full marks

Two discriminations are tested here. First, the arrow must be single-headed — a double-headed arrow (A and D) scores zero. Second, the arrow goes from reactants to products and is labelled ΔH with its actual negative value — it is NOT labelled "−ΔH" (a double-negative). Option C reverses the direction. Only B satisfies both the single-headed requirement and correct direction/label.

Q15 STE §6.3 3 marks · ~6 min

A student draws the following description of their enthalpy level diagram for the combustion of propane (ΔH꜀ = −2220 kJ mol⁻¹): "I drew a y-axis labelled 'energy change'. The products line was drawn above the reactants line. I used a double-headed arrow between the two lines and labelled it '−ΔH = −2220 kJ mol⁻¹'." Identify three errors in the student's diagram. (3 marks)

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:
  1. The y-axis is labelled "energy change" — it must be labelled "enthalpy".
  2. The products line is above the reactants line — for an exothermic combustion reaction, the products line must be below the reactants line.
  3. A double-headed arrow is used — the arrow must be single-headed, going from reactants to products. (Alternatively: the arrow is labelled "−ΔH" — this is a double-negative; it should be labelled "ΔH꜀ = −2220 kJ mol⁻¹".)
Why this scores full marks

Each error identified scores 1 mark. Error 1 targets the mandatory "enthalpy" y-axis label (penalised in Jun 2022 Q18(a)(iii)). Error 2 tests that exothermic reactions have products below reactants. Error 3 tests either the single-headed arrow rule or the "−ΔH" double-negative pitfall — both are valid marks. A student giving any three distinct errors scores full marks.

Q16 EXP §6.3 2 marks · ~4 min

A reaction proceeds via an intermediate. On the enthalpy level diagram, the intermediate has its own horizontal line between the reactants and products. Explain why the overall ΔH arrow must go from the reactant level to the product level, and NOT from the reactant level to the intermediate level.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The overall enthalpy change, ΔH, is defined as the difference in enthalpy between the final products and the initial reactants. The intermediate is a transient species that is neither a starting material nor a final product of the overall reaction. Therefore, the ΔH arrow must span from the initial reactant level to the final product level, representing the net enthalpy change for the overall reaction.

Why this scores full marks

Mark 1: Stating that ΔH is defined between final products and initial reactants (not between reactants and intermediate). Mark 2: Explaining that the intermediate is not a starting material or final product of the overall reaction, so it is not part of the overall enthalpy change.

Q17 STE §6.3 2 marks · ~4 min

A student examining a reaction profile diagram states: "The activation energy of this reaction is the same as the enthalpy change, ΔH." Explain why this statement is incorrect.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The activation energy (Eₐ) is the minimum energy required for reactants to react, represented by the height of the energy barrier (the "hump" on the reaction profile) above the reactant level. The enthalpy change (ΔH) is the overall difference in enthalpy between the products and the reactants, represented by the difference in the horizontal levels. These are different features of the diagram and are not the same quantity.

Why this scores full marks

Mark 1 is for correctly describing what activation energy represents (energy barrier / minimum energy for reaction / the peak of the curve). Mark 2 is for correctly describing what ΔH represents (overall level difference between reactants and products). The student's error is a fundamental confusion between the vertical "hump" (Eₐ) and the horizontal level change (ΔH).

§6.4(i) — Standard Enthalpy Change of Reaction, ΔH°r

Q18 EXP §6.4 2 marks · ~4 min

The standard enthalpy change of reaction for the equation 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l) is −572 kJ mol⁻¹, whereas the standard enthalpy change of reaction for H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → H₂O(l) is −286 kJ mol⁻¹. Explain why the two values are different even though both equations describe the same reaction.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The value of ΔH°r depends on the stoichiometry of the equation as written. The first equation shows the formation of 2 moles of water, so its enthalpy change is twice the value for the formation of 1 mole of water shown in the second equation. This is because enthalpy is an extensive property — it is proportional to the amount of substance reacting.

Why this scores full marks

M1 is awarded for stating that ΔH°r depends on the stoichiometric quantities in the equation. M2 is awarded for explaining the mathematical relationship (the first equation produces twice as much water, so the enthalpy change is doubled). A student who believes both equations should give the same ΔH°r has not understood that the "per mol" in ΔH°r refers to the molar quantities as written in the specific equation, not per mole of a particular substance.

Q19 DIA §6.4 2 marks · ~4 min

The enthalpy level diagram below shows a two-step reaction A → C that proceeds through an intermediate B: - Level of A (reactants): −50 kJ mol⁻¹ - Level of B (intermediate): −180 kJ mol⁻¹ - Level of C (products): −320 kJ mol⁻¹ Arrow X points from level A down to level C (bypassing B). Arrow Y points from level A down to level B. Arrow Z points from level B down to level C. A student is asked to label the overall ΔH for the reaction A → C. Which arrow (X, Y, or Z) should they use, and what value should they write? Explain your reasoning. (2 marks)

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The student should use Arrow X and label it ΔH = −270 kJ mol⁻¹. The overall ΔH for a reaction spans from the initial reactant level to the final product level, passing through (but not ending at) any intermediate. ΔH = final enthalpy − initial enthalpy = (−320) − (−50) = −270 kJ mol⁻¹. Arrow Y (−130 kJ mol⁻¹) represents only the first step (A → B), and Arrow Z (−140 kJ mol⁻¹) represents only the second step (B → C). The overall ΔH must connect reactants directly to products, consistent with Hess's Law.

Why this scores full marks

M1 is awarded for selecting Arrow X and explaining that the overall ΔH arrow spans from reactants to products (not ending at the intermediate). M2 is awarded for the correct calculation: (−320) − (−50) = −270 kJ mol⁻¹.

§6.4(ii) — Standard Enthalpy Change of Formation, ΔH°f

Q20 QFR §6.4 2 marks · ~4 min

State the meaning of the term standard enthalpy change of formation.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The enthalpy change when 1 mole of a compound is formed from its elements in their standard states under standard conditions (100 kPa, 298 K).

Why this scores full marks

This hits both mandatory marking points from the Jun 2024 / Jan 2024 mark scheme. M1 is awarded for "1 mol" or "one mole" of the compound formed — omitting this loses this mark. M2 is awarded for "elements in standard states" — writing "from its constituent elements" alone is NOT sufficient; the phrase "standard states" must appear.

Q21 TDC §6.4 2 marks · ~4 min

A student writes the following definition: "The enthalpy change when a compound is formed from its constituent elements at room temperature and 1 atm." This answer scores zero marks. Which two of the following corrections, if applied, would each independently recover one mark? A. Change "a compound" to "1 mole of a compound" B. Change "1 atm" to "100 kPa" C. Add the phrase "in their standard states" after "constituent elements" D. Change "room temperature" to "298 K"

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

A and C.

Why this scores full marks

From the Jan 2024 MS, the two mark points are: (1) "1 mol" / "one mole" of compound, and (2) "elements in standard states". Correction A restores the "1 mole" requirement (M1). Correction C restores the "elements in standard states" requirement (M2). While corrections B and D would make the definition more accurate regarding standard conditions, the mark scheme does not award separate marks for stating "100 kPa" and "298 K" in a definition question — those are covered by the umbrella term "standard conditions".

Q22 WTE §6.4 2 marks · ~4 min

Write a balanced equation, including state symbols, for the standard enthalpy change of formation of liquid methanol, CH₃OH.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

C(s) + 2H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → CH₃OH(l)

Why this scores full marks

M1 is awarded for the correct balanced equation with elements as reactants. Carbon must be C(s) (solid, its standard state), hydrogen must be H₂(g) (not H(g)), and oxygen must be O₂(g) (not O(g)). M2 is awarded for correct state symbols throughout. Fractional coefficients (½O₂) are required and acceptable. The product must be 1 mole of CH₃OH.

§6.4(iii) — Standard Enthalpy Change of Combustion, ΔH°c

Q23 QFR §6.4 2 marks · ~4 min

State the meaning of the term standard enthalpy change of combustion.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

The enthalpy change when 1 mole of a substance reacts completely with oxygen under standard conditions (100 kPa, 298 K), with all reactants and products in their standard states.

Why this scores full marks

In a thermometric titration, the temperature of the reaction mixture does not reach its true maximum value at any point during the experiment. Explain why, and describe how extrapolation of the graph corrects for this.

Answer & Marking Criteria
Answer:

Heat is continuously lost to the surroundings throughout the experiment. This means the measured temperature at every point is lower than it would be in a perfectly insulated system. The true maximum temperature (and true ΔT) was never actually recorded. By drawing two lines of best fit (before and after the end-point) and extrapolating both back to the time of mixing, the graph method estimates what the temperature change would have been if no heat loss had occurred. The difference between the extrapolated lines at the mixing point gives the true ΔT.

Why this scores full marks

Q79 STE §6.10 3 marks · ~6 min

Answer:

Q81 STE §6.10 2 marks · ~4 min

A student is asked to calculate a mean bond enthalpy from atomisation data. They write: "C₂H₅OH(g) → 2C(g) + 6H(g) + O(g) means ΔHₐₜ = C–C bond enthalpy." Identify two errors in this statement.
